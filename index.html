🔍 1. Is Seattle Getting Sunnier?

No, the data does not show a statistically significant increase in sun. Non-parametric Mann-Kendall testing reveals a flat-to-slightly negative trend in annual sunshine hours since 1950 (p = 0.107, Sen's slope: -13.4 hrs/decade). Strict Clear days have averaged an unmoving ~61–64 days per decade for 76 years.

⚖️ 2. The Definition Dilemma

If you define a sunny day as NOAA Clear (≤30% cloud cover), Seattle averages only ~62 days/year. But if you include partly cloudy days with sun breaks (≤70% cloud cover), the count jumps to ~173 days/year (nearly half the calendar). Both definitions show remarkable multi-decadal stability.

🌧️ 3. Less Rain ≠ More Sun (The Gray Cloud Decoupling)

While annual rainfall in Seattle has trended downward from ~61.5 inches in the 1950s to ~44.9 inches in the 2020s, cloud cover has not decreased proportionately. Seattle frequently experiences dry overcast conditions: marine stratus layers and cool low-level inversions keep skies gray even when storms drop less rain.

🌊 4. The 2026 Super El Niño Impact

El Niño modulates Seattle weather on a 2–7 year cycle. Strong El Niño years average 3,093 hours of sunshine vs. 2,996 hours during Strong La Niña (a spread of ~97 hours). The 2026 Super El Niño gave Seattle intermittent sunny winter breaks, but this is a cyclical oscillation rather than a permanent climate trend.
